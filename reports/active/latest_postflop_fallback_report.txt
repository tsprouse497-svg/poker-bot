Postflop Fallback Report
========================

Fallback: postflop-fallback v1
Composite: composite-preflop-chart-postflop-fallback v1

## What this is, and what it is not

This is a continuity device, not a postflop strategy. There is no postflop chart in this
repo and there will not be one in v1, so a simulated hand that reaches a flop would
otherwise have nothing to ask; this fallback checks whenever checking is free, folds to
a bet, and puts money in on exactly one path - a board on which no holding a villain
could possibly have beats hero, whatever card is still to come.

That path is open on the turn and the river and closed on the flop, where the honest
enumeration costs over a million hand evaluations for one decision. So a flop bet always
takes the pot from this bot.

What that makes a hand: against another copy of itself every postflop street checks
through, so a hand is decided preflop by the committed charts and then settled at
showdown. Nothing downstream may read postflop quality into it, because there is no
postflop play here to have quality.

## The enumeration over engine-legal postflop states

Coverage is proved by enumeration, not by sampling. The legal-action sets below were not
written down: each one is what the engine's own `legal_actions` returned for a postflop
seat, swept over every combination of current bet, hero's street bet, and hero's
remaining stack that the engine can be put in. If the engine ever produces a new action
set, this sweep widens on its own rather than missing it.

Legal-action sets the engine produced: 4
  fold/call               to call   10  (the price to call takes hero's whole stack)
  fold/call/raise         to call   20
  fold/check/bet          to call    0
  fold/check/raise        to call    0

The price to call is capped at what hero holds, so it is the price hero can actually
pay. A hero whose whole stack is the price is Phase 06's short hero restated: the old
form, a stack below the price, cannot happen once the price is capped, and the situation
survives as a price equal to the stack.

The pot in each enumerated state is a construction, not data: it is the 100 chips
villain put in on the street before, plus what the two seats have put in on the street
being played. A pot is the sum of what the seats put in, so those chips had to belong to
somebody, and villain's earlier street is the attribution that describes a real hand.
Nothing in the enumeration reads the pot, so no verdict below depends on it - read it as
a claim about how the hand got here rather than as a measurement.

Every state returned a decision. Not one of them was a bet or a raise, at any street,
because aggression needs a size and this repo has no postflop sizing.

## Where money goes in: the worked call examples

The unbeatable call is the only path in this module that invests, so it is written out
as cards. On a complete board hero can see seven of the 52 - two in hand and five on the
board - which leaves 45 unseen and 990 possible two-card villain holdings. Every one of
them is ranked with the same evaluator that ranks hero's own hand. No hand categories,
no table of nut hands, no sampled subset.

The bar is that no holding beats hero. A tie does not count against calling, because a
chop is not a loss: the pot that gets chopped holds the villain's bet and the money
already in the middle, so facing a bet of B into a pot of P that already contains B, a
hand nothing can beat returns at least half of P + B for a payment of B and the call
gains at least (P - B) / 2. P always exceeds B, because a postflop pot holds the preflop
money too. There is no price in that and no equity estimate, which is the whole reason
this one path is allowed to invest.

The `hero holds` column is the evaluator's own category name. It has no separate
royal-flush category, so a royal flush reads there as a straight flush. The `bad rivers`
column applies to turn rows only, and is the number of river cards after which some
holding beats hero; one is enough to fold.

river, royal flush in hand: Ac Kc on Qc Jc Tc 2d 3h
  Calls, because hero holds a royal flush in clubs; nothing beats a royal flush, and the
  only hand that ties it needs Ac and Kc, both of which hero holds. Of 990 possible
  villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and 0 tie hero.

river, quad aces holding the fourth ace: Ac 2c on As Ah Ad Kc Kd
  Calls, because hero holds four aces with a king; only a straight flush beats quad aces
  and no three board cards share a suit, so no flush exists, and a tie needs the fourth
  ace, which hero holds. Of 990 possible villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and
  0 tie hero.

river, quad nines with the kicker on the board: Kd Qh on 9c 9d 9h 9s Ac
  Calls, because hero's best five cards are the four nines and the ace, all of them on
  the board, so every villain holds the same hand: nothing beats hero and every holding
  chops, and a chop is not a loss - the pot hands back the villain's bet along with a
  share of the money already in the middle. Of 990 possible villain holdings on that
  board, 0 beat hero and 990 tie hero.

river, royal flush on the board: 2d 7h on Ac Kc Qc Jc Tc
  Calls, because the nuts is the board itself, a royal flush, and hero's two cards are
  irrelevant; hero cannot hold that hand as a two-card holding and neither can any
  villain, so the whole table chops and hero's share of the pot is guaranteed. Of 990
  possible villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and 990 tie hero.

river, nut flush that one straight flush beats: Ad Kd on 2d 3d 4d 5h Kc
  Folds, because hero holds the ace-high flush and no villain flush can match it, but 6d
  5d makes 2d 3d 4d 5d 6d, a six-high straight flush; one holding out of 990 is enough
  to fold. Of 990 possible villain holdings on that board, 1 beat hero and 0 tie hero.

### On the turn, where a card is still to come

The turn claim is the stronger one: not 'no holding beats me now' but 'no holding beats
me after any river card'. It decomposes into 46 river checks, one per card that can
complete the board, so hero calls the turn only when every one of them comes back safe.
That is 46 x 990 = 45,540 (river, holding) pairs for a single decision.

hero    board               hero holds          beat   tie  bad rivers   verdict
Ac Kc   Qc Jc Tc 2d         straight flush         0     0           0   calls
Th Jd   9c 8c 7h 6d         straight               0     9          25   folds
Th Jd   9c 8c 7h 6d 2h      straight               0     9           -   calls

turn, royal flush in hand: Ac Kc on Qc Jc Tc 2d
  Calls, because hero already holds a royal flush in clubs, so no river card can beat it
  and none can tie it, because a tie needs Ac and Kc and hero holds both. Of 990
  possible villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and 0 tie hero. Of the 46 cards
  that can complete the board, 0 leave some holding beating hero.

turn, straight a club river would break: Th Jd on 9c 8c 7h 6d
  Folds, because hero holds the jack-high straight and nothing beats it on the board as
  it stands, but two clubs are showing, so a club river would give any two clubs a
  flush; the turn claim has to survive every river card and this one does not. Of 990
  possible villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and 9 tie hero. Of the 46 cards
  that can complete the board, 25 leave some holding beating hero.

river, straight a club river would break: Th Jd on 9c 8c 7h 6d 2h
  Calls, because the river came 2h rather than a club, so no flush is ever possible now,
  nothing beats the jack-high straight, and the hand that folded a turn bet calls a
  river one - the same cards, one card later, and the card that would have broken it
  missed. Of 990 possible villain holdings on that board, 0 beat hero and 9 tie hero.

### Why the flop is not on this list

On the flop two cards are still to come, so the same claim runs over C(47,2) = 1,081
villain holdings against C(45,2) = 990 runouts: 1,070,190 evaluations for a single
decision, against 45,540 on the turn and 990 on the river. That is roughly a thousand
river checks where the turn is roughly forty-six, which is the whole difference between
the two, and it is why the turn is here and the flop is not.

So a flop bet always takes the pot from this bot. That gap is recorded in `backlog.yml`
as POSTFLOP-UNBEATABLE-EARLIER-STREETS rather than approximated, because a sampled
version would turn the one fact this module rests on back into a guess.

## What the composite does over the committed sample hands

Fixture: `data/samples/normalized_hands.json`

One strategy object plays the whole hand: preflop from the committed charts, flop
through river from the fallback. Every recorded action in a hand is a decision point. A
point can only be asked when the hand history records that seat's hole cards, which it
does only for seats that reached showdown, so points belonging to a seat that folded
earlier are skipped rather than guessed at.

These four hands were recorded to exercise the replayer, not to be preflop spots the
six-handed 100bb charts cover, so the chart refuses every one of them. That is the
behavior to want: a refusal carries its own reason code out to the caller instead of
being quietly converted into a check.

## Check one number by hand

Postflop decision points across the committed sample hands: 10.

Open `data/samples/normalized_hands.json`. For each hand, go to `streets` and find the
entries named `flop`, `turn` and `river`, ignoring `preflop`. Count the entries in each
one's `actions` list and add them up. The replayer visits exactly one decision point per
recorded action, so that tally is the number above.

  phase02-heads-up-showdown       flop 2, turn 2, river 2                    6
  phase02-three-way-side-pot      flop 0, turn 0, river 0                    0
  phase02-preflop-fold-out        no postflop streets                        0
  phase02-turn-fold-out           flop 2, turn 2                             4
  total                                                                     10

Counting the file by hand gives 10. Driving the same file through the replayer and the
composite reached 10 postflop decision points. The two agree, and the first of them
needed no code at all.

The two `post_blind` entries that open every preflop street are deliberately not in this
count. They are forced posts rather than decisions, and the replayer never offers them
to a strategy.

## The committed decision audit

`reports/active/latest_postflop_decision_audit.jsonl` holds one line per decision in the
Phase 03 record shape, written with the same serializer as the preflop audit, so the
reader that already checks preflop decisions checks these unchanged.

  from the enumeration                                  72
  from the sample hands (postflop only)                  6
  total lines                                           78

Refusals are counted in this report and are deliberately not written to that file. The
record shape does accept a refusal, but a decision audit whose lines are not all
decisions is a file every future reader has to filter before trusting a count, and the
preflop refusals worth auditing are already Phase 05's own evidence.

Every line was constructed through `DecisionAuditRecord`, which is what proves legality:
it rejects an action outside `legal_actions`, an amount above all-in, and an amount
below the minimum raise target. A decision that was not legal could not have been
written to the file.
